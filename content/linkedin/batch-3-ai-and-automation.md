## Post 2: The "What AI Won't Replace" Post
**Source: Blog 5**

Everyone's talking about what AI will do in CRE.

Here's what it won't do:

It won't build trust with a CFO over a two-year relationship.

It won't read the room when a tenant is bluffing about their timeline.

It won't negotiate a $2M TI package at 11pm before a lease expires.

It won't know that a specific submarket is about to turn because you've walked those blocks for 15 years.

Trust. Negotiation. Intuition. Presence.

These are the things that actually close deals. And they're all deeply human.

So what does AI actually do?

It handles the stuff that eats your day BEFORE you ever get to use those skills. The prospect research. The data synthesis. The first-draft outreach. The follow-up scheduling.

AI doesn't make brokers less important. It makes everything AROUND the broker less important.

The research that took 30 minutes? Two minutes. The generic email you were going to send anyway? Now it's personalized with real data. The follow-up you forgot about? Handled.

We're at the email-in-1995 moment for AI in CRE. The brokers who said "I'll never need email, I do deals on the phone" weren't wrong about the phone. They were wrong about email.

Don't be wrong about AI.

## Post 3: The Getting Started Post
**Source: Blog 5**

If you're a CRE broker curious about AI but don't know where to start, here's my advice:

Don't start with outreach automation.

Start with research automation.

Here's why. Every broker I know spends 20-40 minutes researching a prospect before making contact. Company background, recent news, lease expiration timing, decision-maker identification, portfolio analysis.

That research is valuable. But it's also repetitive. And it follows a pattern.

That pattern is exactly what AI is good at.

Step one: Pick 10 target companies you're already planning to prospect this week.

Step two: Use an AI tool to pull together a research brief on each one. Company size, recent moves, leadership changes, known lease dates, relevant news.

Step three: Compare what you get to what you would have found manually.

You'll notice two things. First, it's 80-90% as good as your manual research. Second, it took a fraction of the time.

That's your starting point. Not replacing your process. Accelerating it.

Once you trust the research, then you move to drafting. Then follow-up sequencing. Then pipeline intelligence.

But research first. It's the lowest risk, highest reward entry point. And it frees up the hours you need to actually do what you're best at: having conversations and closing deals.

What's the first thing you'd want to automate in your prospecting workflow?

## Post 4: The Before/After Post
**Source: Blog 6**

Want to see the difference AI makes in broker outreach?

Same prospect. Same broker. Two emails.

**Email A (Generic):**
"Hi Mike, I'm a tenant rep broker specializing in the Dallas market. We help companies like yours find and negotiate office space. I'd love to set up a 15-minute call to discuss your upcoming real estate needs. Let me know if you're available this week."

**Email B (AI-Assisted Research):**
"Hi Mike, I saw Apex Solutions just closed a Series C and announced plans to grow the Dallas team to 150 by Q3. Congrats. Your current space at 2100 McKinney looks like it's around 12,000 SF, which probably gets tight fast at that growth rate. I helped a similar-stage SaaS company in Uptown navigate that exact transition last year, moving from 11K to 22K SF without disrupting operations during their scaling phase. Happy to share how they approached it if that'd be useful."

One of these gets deleted. The other gets a reply.

The difference isn't writing talent. It's research. Email B is built on data that used to take 30 minutes to assemble. Now it takes two.

The personalization isn't fake. It's informed. And it scales.

Automate the work. Keep the relationship manual.

Which email would you respond to?

## Post 5: The Time Math Post
**Source: Blog 6**

I used to spend 15 minutes researching each prospect before writing an email.

Company background. Recent news. Lease timing. Decision-maker identification. Portfolio review.

Good research. Important research. But 15 minutes per prospect meant I could realistically reach 20-25 new companies a week. And that was on a disciplined week.

Now it takes about 2 minutes per prospect. And the outreach is actually more personalized than before.

Here's what that math looks like over a week:

Old way: 25 prospects x 15 min research = 6+ hours just on research
New way: 25 prospects x 2 min research = less than 1 hour

That's 5 hours back. Every single week.

But here's what actually changed my business. I didn't just save 5 hours. I reinvested them.

Same research hours, more prospects: 25/week became 80-100/week.

Within 90 days, my total prospecting time dropped from 20+ hours a week to about 6. But my pipeline grew 3x.

The fear with automation is always that you'll lose the personal touch. That it becomes spam. That fear is valid. Bad automation IS spam.

But good automation doesn't remove personalization. It builds personalization into the system at a speed you can't match manually.

The brokers who figure this out aren't spending less time on relationships. They're spending more.

How much time do you spend on prospect research each week?

## Post 6: The Closing Line Post
**Source: Blog 6**

You won't be replaced by AI.

You'll be replaced by brokers who use AI.

I know that sounds aggressive. But sit with it for a second.

The broker who uses AI doesn't spend less time on relationships. They spend more. Because they've eliminated the hours of manual research, generic email drafting, and forgotten follow-ups that used to consume their week.

They show up to every conversation better prepared. They reach 3-5x more prospects. Their outreach is more personalized, not less, because it's built on synthesized research instead of a quick Google search between calls.

The competitive advantage isn't the technology. It's what the technology frees you up to do.

More meetings. More conversations. More time understanding a client's actual needs instead of copy-pasting company descriptions into an email template.

The brokers I see winning right now aren't the most technical. They're the ones who recognized a simple truth: the grunt work of prospecting was eating the hours they should have been spending on the craft of brokerage.

AI gave them those hours back.

This isn't about replacing the human element. It's about protecting it. The brokers who adopt AI don't become less human in their approach. They become more human, because they finally have the time to be.

The question isn't whether AI will change CRE brokerage. It's whether you'll be the one using it or competing against it.
