# Why Tenant Rep Brokers Are Leaving Deals on the Table

Here's a number that should keep every tenant rep broker up at night: **in any given metro market, roughly 20% of commercial leases expire within the next 24 months.** That's thousands of tenants who will need representation. Thousands of fees sitting there, waiting.

Now here's the uncomfortable question: how many of those tenants have you actually talked to?

If you're honest — really honest — the answer is somewhere between "not enough" and "barely any." And you're not alone. The vast majority of tenant rep brokers, even productive ones doing $1M+ in GCI, are systematically leaving deals on the table every single quarter. Not because they're lazy. Not because they lack skill. Because the way this business has always been done makes it mathematically impossible to capture even a fraction of the opportunity in front of you.

Let's break down why.

## The Math Problem: You Can't Outwork Arithmetic

Take a mid-sized market — say, Denver. There are roughly 8,000 office tenants occupying 10,000 SF or more. At any given time, about 1,600 of those tenants have leases expiring in the next 18-24 months. That's your addressable market for just one product type in one city.

Now let's look at your week. You're a solid producer. You set aside two hours a day for prospecting — which, let's be real, is more than most brokers actually do. In those two hours, you might research 8-10 companies, find the right contact, craft a semi-personalized email, maybe make a few calls. On a great week, you're doing meaningful outreach to 40-50 prospects.

**Fifty prospects a week out of 1,600 that need you right now.**

At that pace, it would take you 32 weeks to reach every expiring tenant once. By the time you get to prospect #800, half of the early ones have already re-signed, relocated, or hired your competitor. And that's assuming you're only focused on office. Add industrial, retail, flex — your addressable universe doubles or triples, but your calendar doesn't.

This isn't a hustle problem. It's a physics problem. There are only so many hours in a day, and the manual work of prospecting eats most of them before you ever get to do the thing you're actually great at: negotiating deals.

## The Timing Problem: Second Place Pays Zero

Tenant rep isn't like listing. You don't put up a sign and wait for the phone to ring. You're in a foot race, and the starting gun goes off the moment a tenant begins thinking about their lease — which is typically 12-18 months before expiration.

The broker who gets there first has a massive structural advantage. Not just because of the relationship, but because tenants are busy running their businesses. They don't want to interview five brokers. They want someone credible to show up at the right moment, demonstrate they understand the situation, and take the problem off their plate.

Consider this scenario: Sarah runs a 40-person SaaS company in Austin. Her lease expires in 14 months. She mentioned it at a board meeting, and it's been on her mental to-do list, but she hasn't done anything about it. Then a broker emails her — not a generic blast, but something specific. He references her company's recent Series B, notes that her current building has limited parking (which he knows because he checked Google Earth), and suggests she might want to explore options in the Domain area where three of her competitors have relocated.

Sarah takes the call. She signs an exclusive. Deal done.

Meanwhile, two other brokers had Sarah on their "list." One was going to reach out next month. The other had her in a CRM but never followed up after the first email bounced. They'll never know how close they were.

**In tenant rep, timing isn't everything. It's the only thing.** And if your outreach system is manual, your timing is random. You get lucky sometimes. But luck isn't a strategy.

## The Follow-Up Problem: One and Done Doesn't Get It Done

Let's talk about the most predictable failure pattern in broker prospecting.

A broker spends 15 minutes researching a prospect. Crafts a thoughtful email. Hits send. Feels good about it. Then... nothing. No response. The broker moves on to the next prospect, telling themselves they'll circle back.

They won't.

The data on this is brutal. **Research across B2B sales consistently shows that 80% of deals require 5-12 touches before a prospect engages.** Yet the average broker sends 1.2 outreach attempts before abandoning a prospect. Read that again: 1.2.

You're putting in the hardest work — the research, the initial outreach — and then quitting right before the payoff. It's like training for a marathon and dropping out at mile 20.

The brokers who clean up in tenant rep aren't necessarily smarter or more connected. They're more persistent. They have systems that ensure no prospect falls through the cracks. They follow up at day 3, day 10, day 21, and day 45. They vary the channel — email, then LinkedIn, then a call, then a handwritten note. They add value each time instead of just "checking in."

But here's the catch-22: if you can barely reach 50 new prospects a week, how are you supposed to simultaneously maintain multi-touch campaigns on the 200+ prospects you've already contacted? The math breaks again.

## The Personalization Problem: Generic Gets Deleted

Every tenant rep broker knows that personalized outreach outperforms generic templates. It's not even close. A message that references a company's specific situation — their lease timeline, recent news, growth trajectory, pain points with their current space — converts at 3-5x the rate of a "Hi, I'm a broker, let me know if you need space" email.

The problem is obvious: personalization takes time. Real personalization — not just swapping in a company name with a merge field — requires research.

You need to know when their lease expires. What they're paying. Whether they've raised funding or announced layoffs. What their Glassdoor reviews say about the office. Whether they've been in the news. What their headcount trend looks like on LinkedIn. Whether the CEO just posted about returning to office.

Synthesizing all of that into a compelling 150-word email takes a good broker 10-15 minutes per prospect. At scale, you're choosing between two bad options: send personalized emails to a tiny number of prospects, or send generic emails to a larger number and watch your response rate crater.

Most brokers compromise in the worst possible way — they send semi-personalized emails to a medium-sized list and get medium results. Not terrible enough to change, not good enough to break through.

## The Data Problem: Drowning in Information, Starving for Insight

This might be the most frustrating piece. Brokers today have access to more data than at any point in history. CoStar gives you lease comps and expirations. LinkedIn shows you org charts and hiring trends. Google Alerts can track company news. Your CRM has years of contact history.

The data is there. What's missing is the synthesis.

Take a concrete example. Let's say you pull a CoStar report showing 300 lease expirations in your submarket over the next 18 months. Great. Now what? You need to cross-reference those tenants against LinkedIn to find the decision-maker. Check the news to see if any are growing, shrinking, or being acquired. Look at their current space to understand potential pain points. Figure out which ones are already represented and which are fair game.

That's not a morning's work. That's a week's work. And by the time you've done it for those 300 tenants, you need to do it again because the data has changed.

**The brokers who are winning have figured out that data without speed is just trivia.** It's interesting but not actionable. The gap between "knowing" and "doing" is where deals die.

## How the Top 1% Are Solving This

Here's what's changing — and if you're paying attention, you've already noticed it.

The highest-producing tenant rep brokers and the fastest-growing brokerages aren't just working harder. They're building systems that fundamentally change the math.

They're using AI-driven platforms that can synthesize data from multiple sources — lease expirations, company news, hiring patterns, real estate filings — and automatically generate personalized outreach at scale. Not robotic, mail-merge "personalization." Actually intelligent messaging that reads like a human broker wrote it, because a human broker guided the strategy and the technology executed it.

They're deploying automated multi-touch sequences that ensure every qualified prospect gets 5-7 touchpoints across email, LinkedIn, and other channels — without requiring the broker to manually manage hundreds of follow-up tasks.

They're compressing the time between "a lease is expiring" and "a personalized message lands in the decision-maker's inbox" from weeks to hours.

And they're doing all of this not to replace the broker, but to get the broker to the table faster. Because once you're sitting across from a tenant, once you're walking spaces and negotiating LOIs, no algorithm can replace you. That's where your expertise, your relationships, and your market knowledge create irreplaceable value.

The irony is that technology isn't making the broker less important. It's making everything *around* the broker less important — the busy work, the data entry, the follow-up tracking, the manual research. Strip all that away, and what's left is the highest-value version of what you do.

## The Uncomfortable Bottom Line

If you're a tenant rep broker reading this, here's the honest truth: your current outreach process is probably leaving 90%+ of your potential market untouched. Not because you're failing, but because the old way of doing this work has a hard ceiling that no amount of effort can break through.

The question isn't whether the industry is going to change. It's whether you'll be the broker who changed first — or the one who wonders where all the deals went.

The math is there. The technology is there. The only variable left is whether you're willing to look at your process and admit that what got you here won't get you where you want to go.

Because right now, someone in your market is reaching the tenants you haven't gotten to yet. And they're not working harder than you. They're just working differently.
