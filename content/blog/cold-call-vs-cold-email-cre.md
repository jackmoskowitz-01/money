# Cold Call vs. Cold Email for CRE Brokers: What Actually Works in 2026

Every brokerage has them — the cold-call purists and the email-first crew. The senior broker who swears the phone is the only real tool. The younger associate who hasn't made a cold call in six months and is booking meetings off email sequences.

They're both right. And they're both leaving money on the table.

The honest answer to "should I call or email?" is: it depends on the prospect, the situation, and the quality of your data. The best brokers in 2026 aren't picking sides. They're using both channels strategically, in sequence, and letting each one make the other more effective.

This post breaks down what's actually working — with real numbers, a practical framework, and the hybrid approach that top-producing tenant reps are running every week.

---

## Cold Calling in 2026: What's Changed

Let's start with the uncomfortable truth: cold calling is harder than it was five years ago. Answer rates across B2B have dropped significantly, and commercial real estate is no exception. But "harder" doesn't mean "dead." It means the bar for doing it well has gone up.

### The Pros

- **Immediate feedback.** You know within 30 seconds whether someone is interested or not. No waiting three days to see if they opened your email. No wondering if it went to spam.

- **Rapport builds fast.** A 90-second phone conversation creates more trust than a dozen emails. When a prospect hears your voice and picks up on your market knowledge, that's hard to replicate in text.

- **Harder to ignore than email.** A ringing phone demands a decision. An email can sit unread for weeks.

- **Works great for warm and lukewarm leads.** When someone has already opened your email or been referred to you, a phone call converts interest into a meeting faster than another email ever will.

### The Cons

- **Answer rates have dropped to 5-8%.** For every 100 dials, you're getting 5-8 live conversations. That's a lot of rejection per hour.

- **Voicemail is a black hole.** Most voicemails don't get returned. Don't count on a callback.

- **It's time-intensive per prospect.** Between researching, dialing, navigating phone trees, and leaving voicemails, you're spending 3-4 minutes per attempt even when nobody answers.

- **Hard to scale.** There's a ceiling on how many calls one broker can make in a day before quality drops.

### When Cold Calling Works Best

- Follow-ups after email opens or clicks
- Referral-based outreach ("Sarah Chen mentioned I should give you a call...")
- High-value A-tier prospects where the deal size justifies the time investment
- Local and smaller companies where the CEO or founder actually answers the phone
- Situations where speed matters — a lease expiration is 12 months out and you need to get in front of them now

## Cold Email in 2026: What's Changed

Email has changed more than calling in the last few years, mostly because of AI. The tools are better. The personalization is better. But so is everyone else's, which means the bar has moved here too.

### The Pros

- **Scalable.** You can reach 50-100 prospects in a day with personalized emails. Try doing that on the phone.

- **Trackable.** Open rates, click rates, reply rates — you know exactly what's working. You can see who opened your email three times at 7 AM, which tells you something a cold call never would.

- **Works asynchronously.** Your email lands at 6:30 AM and gets read during the prospect's morning coffee. The email works while you sleep.

- **Personalization at scale with AI.** AI tools can now research a prospect's company, lease situation, and market context — then generate a genuinely personalized email in seconds. What used to take 15 minutes per prospect now takes moments.

- **Creates a paper trail.** When a prospect forwards your email to their CFO, your value proposition travels with it. Calls don't do that.

### The Cons

- **Inbox competition is fierce.** Decision-makers receive hundreds of emails a day. Subject lines matter more than ever.

- **Deliverability challenges.** If your domain reputation isn't strong, your emails might not reach the inbox. Sending cold emails from your main brokerage domain without proper warmup can burn it.

- **Easy to ignore.** Opening an email and deleting it takes half a second. There's no social pressure to respond the way there is on a phone call.

- **Can feel impersonal.** If your personalization is shallow — just dropping their company name into a generic pitch — it actually hurts more than it helps.

### When Cold Email Works Best

- First touches with cold prospects you've never spoken to
- Large prospect lists where you need to cast a wide net efficiently
- Companies where you don't have a direct phone number for the decision-maker
- Complex or large organizations where you need to establish credibility before asking for a meeting
- Markets where you want to build awareness over time through a [follow-up sequence](/blog/tenant-rep-prospecting-playbook)

## The Hybrid Approach: What Top Brokers Actually Do

The best tenant rep brokers don't think in terms of "call or email." They think in terms of sequences — a multi-touch cadence that uses each channel at the right moment.

Here's a proven five-touch sequence that consistently outperforms single-channel outreach:

**Day 1: Personalized email.** Reference something specific — their lease timeline, a market trend affecting their submarket, a recent company announcement. Keep it under 100 words. One clear ask. ([See our CRE email templates for examples.](/blog/commercial-real-estate-cold-email-templates))

**Day 3-4: Phone call.** Reference the email directly: "Hi Mark, I sent you a note earlier this week about the Plano office market — wanted to put a voice to the name." This call has context. It's warmer. It's not a pure cold call anymore.

**Day 7: Follow-up email with new information.** Don't just "check in." Add value. Share a market stat, a comparable lease, or a relevant insight they haven't seen. Give them a reason to reply beyond politeness.

**Day 14: LinkedIn message.** Short, casual, and different in tone from your emails. "Hey Sarah — been following your company's growth. Would love to share some data on where the market's headed for spaces your size. Worth a quick chat?"

**Day 21: Final call or break-up email.** Either try the phone one more time with a direct ask, or send a brief "closing the loop" email that gives them an easy out while leaving the door open.

The logic is simple: **the email warms up the call, and the call adds urgency to the email.** A prospect who's seen your name in their inbox is 3-4x more likely to take your call. And a prospect who's spoken to you briefly is far more likely to open your next email. Together, they're roughly 3x more effective than either channel alone.

## When to Lead with a Call vs. an Email

Not every prospect should get the same sequence. Here's a quick framework for deciding which channel comes first:

### Lead with a Call When:

- **You have a referral.** "David Park suggested I reach out" is the most powerful opening line in prospecting. Don't waste it on an email — pick up the phone.
- **There's a hot trigger.** They just lost their broker, their lease expires in 12 months, or they announced an expansion. Urgency favors the phone.
- **It's a small company.** At companies under 50 employees, the decision-maker often answers their own phone. Take advantage of that.
- **You have a direct number.** If you've got a cell phone number, use it. That's a valuable asset — don't squander it on an email when a call will be 5x more impactful.

### Lead with an Email When:

- **You're doing cold outreach at scale.** If you're working a list of 200 prospects this quarter, you need email to cover ground efficiently.
- **You don't have a phone number.** Sometimes all you have is an email address. Work with what you've got.
- **It's a large or complex organization.** At bigger companies, you may need to establish credibility before you earn a phone conversation. An insightful email that gets forwarded internally does that work for you.
- **You need to explain something nuanced.** If your value proposition depends on market data, comps, or a specific insight about their situation, email gives you the space to lay that out clearly.

## The Phone Script That Works

When you do pick up the phone — especially as a follow-up to an email — here's a script framework that keeps it natural and under 30 seconds:

> "Hi [Name], this is [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. I sent you a quick note earlier this week about [specific topic — e.g., 'what's happening with office rents in the Uptown submarket']. I work with companies like yours on the tenant side, and I had a couple of insights on your area that I thought might be useful. Would it make sense to grab 15 minutes this week so I can share what I'm seeing?"

That's it. No pitch about your brokerage's history. No listing of services. Just three things:

1. **Identify yourself and reference the email** (so they know you're not a random caller)
2. **Offer specific value** (market insight, not a sales pitch)
3. **Ask for a small commitment** (15 minutes, not an hour-long meeting)

If they say "not right now," respond with: "Totally understand. Mind if I send over a quick market snapshot for your area? That way you'll have it when the timing's right." Now you've earned permission to follow up by email, and the next touch will be expected, not cold.

## How AI Changes the Equation

AI hasn't replaced cold calls. The human voice, the real conversation, the ability to read tone and pivot in real time — that's still irreplaceable.

What AI has done is make the email side of the equation dramatically better. Personalization that used to take 15 minutes of research now happens in seconds. Follow-up sequences can adapt based on prospect behavior — someone who opened your email three times gets a different Day 3 message than someone who never opened it.

And here's the compounding effect: **when your emails are significantly better, your calls become more effective too.** By the time you pick up the phone, the prospect has already received a sharp, relevant email with your name on it. You're not cold anymore. You're lukewarm at worst.

The brokers who are winning right now aren't choosing between the phone and the inbox. They're using AI to make their email outreach so good that every subsequent call is warmer and more likely to land a meeting. That's the real advantage — not replacing the work, but making every touch count.

If you're spending hours on outreach and still not filling your pipeline, [the problem might not be your effort — it might be your process](/blog/tenant-rep-brokers-leaving-deals-on-table). The tools exist to fix that. The question is whether you're using them.
