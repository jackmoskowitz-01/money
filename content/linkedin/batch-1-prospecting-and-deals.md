## Post 4: The 21-Day Cadence Post

**Links to:** "The Tenant Rep Broker's Prospecting Playbook"

```
Most tenant rep brokers don't have a prospecting system.

They have a to-do list they feel guilty about.

Here's a simple 21-day outreach cadence that actually works:

Day 1 → LinkedIn connection request (with a note — not the default message)
Day 3 → First email. Short. Reference their lease timeline or a market insight.
Day 7 → Phone call. Yes, the phone still works.
Day 10 → Second email. New angle. Maybe a recent comp or submarket trend.
Day 14 → LinkedIn message. Share something useful — an article, a data point.
Day 21 → Break-up email. Let them know the door's open, then move on.

6 touches. 3 channels. 21 days.

Most brokers stop after touch one. The ones running a cadence like this are booking 2-3x more meetings from the same prospect list.

The key: every touch needs to add value. Not "just checking in." Not "circling back." Something specific to their situation.
```

## Post 5: The 5 Mistakes Post

**Links to:** "The Tenant Rep Broker's Prospecting Playbook"

```
5 prospecting mistakes I see tenant rep brokers make over and over:

1. Treating prospecting like a one-touch activity.
You send one email, get no reply, and move on. But most deals take 5-12 touches. One attempt isn't prospecting — it's a lottery ticket.

2. Leading with yourself instead of the prospect.
"We're a full-service brokerage with 20 years of experience..." Nobody cares. Lead with their problem. Their lease expiration. Their submarket.

3. No system for follow-up.
If your follow-up strategy lives in your head, it doesn't exist. The brokers who win have a repeatable cadence they run every single week.

4. Spending 15 minutes researching a prospect you'll never contact again.
Deep research is great — if you actually follow through. Most brokers over-research and under-execute. Batch your research. Streamline it. Or automate it.

5. Ignoring the channels where your prospects actually are.
Email-only prospecting is leaving meetings on the table. The best results come from combining email, phone, and LinkedIn in a coordinated sequence.
```

## Post 6: The Metrics Post

**Links to:** "The Tenant Rep Broker's Prospecting Playbook"

```
Brokers love market data.

But most have zero data on their own prospecting.

Here are the benchmarks top-performing tenant rep brokers are hitting:

Outreach volume
→ 50-75 personalized touches per week (across email, phone, LinkedIn)

Email response rate
→ 15-25% is strong. Below 10%, your messaging needs work.

Meeting conversion
→ 3-5% of total prospects should convert to a meeting. If you're below 2%, look at your targeting or your follow-up cadence.

Touches to meeting
→ Average of 6-8 touches before booking. If you're giving up after 2, you're leaving meetings on the table.

Pipeline coverage
→ You need 3-4x your revenue goal in active pipeline to hit your number.

Most brokers I talk to can tell me their market's vacancy rate to the decimal. But they can't tell me their own response rate, their average touches per prospect, or how many meetings they booked last month.

You can't improve what you don't measure.
```
