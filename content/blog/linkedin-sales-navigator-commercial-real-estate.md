# How to Use LinkedIn Sales Navigator for Commercial Real Estate Prospecting

Most commercial real estate brokers have a LinkedIn profile. Very few use it to prospect. And almost none are using LinkedIn Sales Navigator — the platform's paid prospecting tool — in a way that consistently generates new business.

That's a missed opportunity. Especially for tenant rep brokers, because tenant rep is fundamentally a people business, not a property business. You're not trying to find a building. You're trying to find the CFO who's about to outgrow their office, the VP of Operations hired to consolidate three locations, or the Head of Real Estate at a tech company that raised a Series C and hasn't signed a lease yet.

Those people are on LinkedIn. And Sales Navigator is built to help you find them, track them, and start conversations with them. Yet every Sales Navigator tutorial out there is written for SaaS salespeople. The strategy is completely different when you're a broker identifying companies that need space.

This guide fixes that.

---

## Why LinkedIn Sales Navigator for CRE

Most brokers use LinkedIn passively — scrolling, accepting connections, maybe posting once a quarter. They treat it as a digital rolodex, not a prospecting engine.

Sales Navigator changes the equation. It gives you three things free LinkedIn doesn't:

**Advanced search filters.** Filter by company size, geography, industry, seniority, job title, and more. Build a hyper-targeted list of decision-makers in your market in minutes.

**Lead tracking and alerts.** Save prospects and get notified when they change jobs, post content, get mentioned in the news, or when their company makes a hiring push. These are intent signals that tell you when someone might need space.

**InMail access.** Message anyone on LinkedIn, even if you're not connected. For cold outreach to C-suite prospects who ignore email, this is valuable.

The core insight: tenant rep prospecting is about finding the right people at the right companies at the right time. Sales Navigator is purpose-built for exactly that.

## Setting Up Your Account

### Which Plan to Choose

For most individual brokers, **Sales Navigator Core** is the right choice at roughly $80 per month (less annually). It includes advanced search, lead and account saving, InMail credits (typically 50 per month), and real-time alerts. The Advanced and Advanced Plus tiers add team features and CRM integrations for larger brokerages. Start with Core. You can always upgrade.

### Optimize Your Profile First

Before you start prospecting, fix your profile. Every time you view someone's profile or send an InMail, they're going to look at yours. It's your landing page.

The biggest mistake brokers make: their headline says their title.

"Senior Vice President at XYZ Brokerage" tells a prospect nothing about what you do for them. Compare that to:

*"I help growing tech companies in Austin find the right office space — and negotiate leases that save them money."*

That headline immediately communicates value. When a prospect sees that you viewed their profile, they understand what you do and why it might matter to them.

A few more profile optimizations:

- **Banner image:** Use a branded image or a photo of your market's skyline. Not the default blue gradient.
- **About section:** Write it for your prospects, not your peers. Explain who you help, what problems you solve, and what the outcome looks like.
- **Featured section:** Pin a market report, a case study, or a short post about a recent deal. This builds credibility before you ever send a message.
- **Activity:** Post something at least once a week. It doesn't have to be long. A quick market observation, a lease comp insight, or a comment on a trend. Prospects will check your activity to see if you're actually engaged in the industry.

## Building Saved Searches for CRE Prospects

This is where Sales Navigator earns its subscription fee. The search functionality is the core of your prospecting workflow.

### Key Filters for Tenant Rep Brokers

When you open a Lead search in Sales Navigator, you'll see a panel of filters on the left. Here are the ones that matter most for CRE:

**Job Title.** This is your primary filter. You're looking for the people who make or influence real estate decisions. Common titles to search:
- CEO, President, Founder (especially at companies with 20-200 employees where the CEO is involved in the lease)
- CFO, VP of Finance (they approve the budget)
- VP of Operations, COO, Head of Operations (they manage the physical footprint)
- Head of Real Estate, Director of Real Estate, VP of Real Estate (at larger companies with dedicated real estate roles)
- VP of People, Head of HR, Chief People Officer (increasingly involved in office decisions post-hybrid)

**Company Headcount.** A strong proxy for space needs:
- 11-50: startups approaching the point where they need dedicated space
- 51-200: the sweet spot for mid-market tenant rep
- 201-500: larger tenants with more complex requirements
- 501-1000+: enterprise-level, usually have internal real estate teams

**Geography.** Filter by metro area, city, or state. Sales Navigator uses the person's listed location, so search the metro you serve.

**Industry.** Narrow by vertical if you specialize. Technology, healthcare, financial services, professional services, and manufacturing are common tenant rep target industries.

### Example Search: Building Your Ideal Prospect List

Let's walk through a real example.

Say you're a tenant rep broker in Austin, TX, focused on mid-market tech companies. Here's how you'd build the search:

1. Open Sales Navigator and click **Lead filters**
2. Set **Title**: CFO
3. Set **Company headcount**: 51-200
4. Set **Geography**: Austin, Texas Metropolitan Area
5. Set **Industry**: Technology, Information and Internet, Software Development

*What you'll see: a list of CFOs at mid-size tech companies in Austin. Depending on your market, this might return 50-300 results — a highly targeted prospect list that would have taken days to build manually.*

Now here's the key step: **save this search.** Click "Save search" in the top right. Give it a clear name like "Austin Tech CFOs 51-200."

Once saved, Sales Navigator will send you weekly email alerts when new people match this search. That means every time a new CFO joins a mid-size Austin tech company, you'll know about it. That's a prospecting trigger handed to you automatically.

### Build Multiple Searches

Don't stop at one. Build a library of saved searches that cover your target market:

- "Austin Tech CEOs 11-50" (startups about to need space)
- "Austin CFOs 201-500 All Industries" (larger deals)
- "Austin VP Operations 51-500 Healthcare" (if healthcare is a target vertical)
- "Austin Head of Real Estate 500+" (large enterprise contacts)

Each search becomes a living prospect list that updates itself.

## Using Lead Lists to Organize Prospects

Saved searches find people. Lead Lists help you organize and work them systematically.

### Create Tiered Lists

Set up lists that reflect your prospect prioritization:

- **A-List: High Priority** — Companies you know are in the market, have expiring leases, or match your ideal client profile perfectly. These get the most outreach attention.
- **B-List: Warm Prospects** — Good fit companies with no confirmed trigger. You're watching for signals.
- **C-List: Long-Term Nurture** — Companies that could become prospects in 12-24 months. You'll check in quarterly.

When you find a strong prospect in a saved search, add them to the appropriate list. This takes one click in Sales Navigator.

### Track Your Activity

Sales Navigator shows a timestamp for when you last contacted or viewed each lead. Use this to make sure you're not letting good prospects go cold. A simple weekly review of your A-List — who have I reached out to, who needs a follow-up, who hasn't responded — keeps your pipeline moving.

### Monitor Prospect Activity

This is one of Sales Navigator's most underrated features. When you save a lead, you'll see their activity in your Sales Navigator feed:

- When they post or share content (opportunity to engage)
- When they change roles (major trigger)
- When they're mentioned in the news
- When their company posts job openings (growth signal)

Think of your Lead Lists as a living dashboard of your target market's activity.

## Spotting Intent Signals

Generic outreach gets ignored. Timely outreach gets responses. Sales Navigator helps you identify the timing signals — what salespeople call "intent signals" — that tell you a company might need space soon.

### Job Changes

The single most powerful signal in tenant rep prospecting. When a company hires a new CFO, COO, or VP of Operations, there's a high probability they'll review the real estate portfolio within 90 days. Sales Navigator alerts you when saved leads change jobs. Move fast — a broker who reaches out in their first month has an enormous advantage over one who calls six months later.

### Hiring Surges

If you see a company posting 20, 30, or 50 open positions on LinkedIn, that's an expansion signal. They're going to need more space. Sales Navigator's company insights show headcount growth trends — look for companies growing 15%+ year-over-year in your market.

### Funding and Acquisition News

Sales Navigator's news feed surfaces funding rounds, acquisitions, and major company announcements for your saved leads and accounts. A Series B or C raise almost always means growth. An acquisition often means consolidation or relocation. Both are tenant rep opportunities.

### Content Signals

Pay attention to what your prospects post about. If a CEO is posting about hybrid work, return-to-office policies, company culture, or scaling their team — they're thinking about space, even if they don't realize it yet. These posts are also perfect conversation starters for your outreach.

## The Outreach Sequence with Sales Navigator

You've built your searches. You've organized your lists. You've spotted a trigger. Now what?

Don't lead with a pitch. Lead with presence. Here's a five-step sequence that works:

### Step 1: View Their Profile

When you view someone's profile, they get a notification. If your profile is optimized, they'll see your name, headline, and what you do. Low-pressure first impression. Do this 2-3 days before you reach out.

### Step 2: Connect with a Personalized Note

Send a connection request with a short note. Not a pitch. Something like:

*"Hi Sarah — I saw your recent move to [Company]. I work with a lot of growing tech companies in Austin on their real estate strategy. Would love to connect and be a resource if the topic ever comes up."*

That's it. No ask. No meeting request. Just relevance and warmth.

### Step 3: Engage with Their Content

If they post on LinkedIn, like it. Leave a thoughtful comment. Share it if it's relevant to your audience. This builds familiarity. When you eventually send a more direct message, you're not a stranger — you're that broker who's been engaging with their content.

### Step 4: Send a Value-First InMail or Email

After you've established some presence, send a message with a genuine insight. A market stat. A comp that's relevant to their area. A trend affecting their industry. Something that demonstrates you actually know their world. More on InMail best practices below.

### Step 5: Go Multi-Channel

LinkedIn alone isn't enough. Find their email and phone number, then layer in email and calls. A prospect who saw your profile view, read your InMail, and then gets a brief voicemail is far more likely to respond than one who just got a cold email.

## InMail Best Practices for CRE Brokers

InMail is your direct messaging tool within Sales Navigator. You get a limited number each month, so use them wisely. Here's what works:

### Keep It Under 100 Words

Seriously. Long InMails don't get read. Your prospect is scanning this on their phone between meetings. Get to the point.

### Lead with Insight, Not Your Resume

Don't open with who you are. Open with something relevant to them.

Bad: *"Hi, I'm a Senior VP at ABC Brokerage with 15 years of experience in the Austin market..."*

Good: *"Hi Sarah — I noticed [Company] has added about 40 roles in Austin over the last quarter. The submarket you're in is tightening up, and a few of the better blocks of space are getting spoken for."*

The first is about you. The second is about them.

### One Clear Call to Action

Don't ask for a 30-minute meeting. Ask for something smaller. "Would a quick market snapshot for your submarket be useful?" or "Happy to send over what I'm seeing in terms of availability in your area — want me to?"

### Sample InMail Template

Here's a template you can adapt:

> Hi [First Name] —
>
> Noticed [Company] has been growing in [City] — congrats on the momentum. I've been tracking the [submarket] pretty closely, and there's been some interesting movement on the availability side that could affect your options over the next 6-12 months.
>
> Happy to share a quick snapshot if it'd be useful. No strings attached.
>
> Best,
> [Your name]

That's 58 words. It's relevant, it's low-pressure, and it gives them a reason to respond.

## Advanced Tips

Once you're comfortable with the basics, these techniques will help you get more out of Sales Navigator.

### Boolean Search Strings for CRE

Sales Navigator's title filter supports Boolean logic. Use this to run more precise searches:

- **"VP Operations" OR "Director Operations" OR "Head of Operations"** — catches variations of the same role
- **"Real Estate" AND NOT "Agent"** — finds corporate real estate professionals while filtering out residential agents
- **"CEO" OR "Founder" OR "President"** — casts a wide net for top decision-makers at smaller companies

You can also use Boolean in the keyword search to find people who mention real estate in their profile even if it's not their title: **"office space" OR "lease" OR "headquarter" OR "expansion"** will surface people who've mentioned these terms in their profile or posts.

### Using TeamLink for Warm Introductions

If your brokerage has multiple people on Sales Navigator, TeamLink shows you when a colleague is connected to one of your prospects. A warm introduction from a mutual connection converts at a dramatically higher rate than a cold InMail. Before you cold-message a high-value prospect, always check TeamLink first.

### Exporting for CRM Integration

Sales Navigator doesn't let you export lead lists directly to CSV. But you can use the native Salesforce or HubSpot integrations, manually log prospects as you work your lists, or use third-party sync tools. The key: Sales Nav is where you find and monitor prospects, but your CRM should be your system of record.

### Tracking ROI on Your Subscription

At roughly $1,000 per year, Sales Navigator needs to generate just one deal to pay for itself many times over. Track the numbers: prospects added per week, InMail response rate, conversations that become meetings, meetings that become active pursuits. If you're adding 20 targeted prospects weekly and getting a 15-20% InMail response rate, the ROI is clear.

## Putting It All Together

Here's what a practical weekly Sales Navigator routine looks like for a tenant rep broker:

**Monday (15 minutes):** Review your saved search alerts. Add new matches to the appropriate Lead Lists. Note any job changes or hiring signals.

**Tuesday-Thursday (10 minutes/day):** Work through your A-List. View profiles. Send connection requests. Engage with content. Send 3-5 InMails to prospects with active triggers.

**Friday (10 minutes):** Review your Lead Lists. Move prospects between tiers based on activity. Log any new conversations in your CRM.

That's less than an hour per week. But it's a focused, targeted hour that builds a pipeline of prospects you'd never find by scrolling your LinkedIn feed or waiting for referrals.
