# The CRE Broker's Weekly Outreach Routine: A Hour-by-Hour Breakdown

Every tenant rep broker has the same 168 hours each week. The ones filling their pipeline consistently aren't working more hours than you. They're just spending five of those hours differently.

This is a weekly routine you can copy into your calendar this afternoon and start running tomorrow morning. It covers exactly what to do during each prospecting block — who to target, what to send, when to call, and how to track it all so nothing falls through the cracks.

Print this out. Tape it next to your monitor. Follow it for four weeks and see what happens.

---

## Why Most Brokers Don't Prospect Consistently

The reason most brokers don't prospect enough isn't laziness. They're grinding on active deals, touring spaces, negotiating LOIs, handling client fires. The real problem is structure.

When prospecting doesn't have a dedicated calendar slot, it loses to whatever's urgent. And something is always urgent — a comp analysis due by noon, a landlord counter to respond to, a pipeline update for the Monday meeting. None of those are unimportant. But none of them fill your pipeline six months from now.

The result is the boom-bust cycle: close a deal, realize the pipeline is thin, go on a two-week prospecting blitz, book a few meetings, get consumed by those deals, stop prospecting, repeat. It's exhausting, and it caps your income.

The fix isn't motivation. It's a system — a weekly routine that makes prospecting as non-negotiable as a client meeting.

---

## The Weekly Framework

Five blocks per week, one per day, Monday through Friday. Each block is one hour, except Friday which is 30 minutes. That's 4.5 hours per week — enough to keep a consistent pipeline if you use the time well.

**The key rules:**

1. **Morning blocks only.** Schedule for 8:00–9:00 AM or 9:00–10:00 AM. Before the inbox takes over. If you put prospecting at 3:00 PM, it will get bumped 80% of the time.

2. **Protect the block like a client meeting.** If someone wants that slot, the answer is "I'm booked." Because you are.

3. **Each day has a theme.** This eliminates the "what should I do?" paralysis that kills most sessions before they start.

4. **Track everything.** If you're not measuring it, you're guessing.

Here's the week at a glance:

| Day | Focus | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Pipeline Review + Target List Building | 1 hour |
| Tuesday | Research + First Touches | 1 hour |
| Wednesday | Phone Block | 1 hour |
| Thursday | Follow-Up Sequences | 1 hour |
| Friday | Measure + Plan | 30 minutes |

Now let's break each day down.

---

## Monday: Pipeline Review + Target List Building (1 Hour)

**8:00 AM – 8:20 AM: Pipeline Review**

Open your CRM first thing. Not your email — your CRM. Ask three questions about each prospect:

- **When was the last touch?** More than two weeks with no activity? Schedule a follow-up this week or move them to a nurture sequence.
- **What's the next action?** "Waiting to hear back" isn't a next step — it's a wish. What will you do if they don't respond by Wednesday?
- **Is this still real?** If a prospect has gone dark after four touches and their lease doesn't expire for three years, move them to your long-term nurture list and free up mental bandwidth.

This review keeps your pipeline honest and prevents the trap of 200 "prospects" in your CRM that haven't been touched in months.

**8:20 AM – 8:45 AM: Pull New Data**

Pull fresh lease expiration data from CoStar, CompStak, or whatever your brokerage subscribes to. Look for companies with leases expiring in the 12–24 month window — far enough out that they haven't committed yet, close enough that it's real.

Also check for trigger events: funding announcements, executive hires, expansion news, M&A activity. Any of these can signal a space need that creates an opening for conversation.

Your goal: add 15–20 new prospects to your outreach queue for the week.

**8:45 AM – 9:00 AM: Prioritize by Tier**

Not all prospects are created equal. Tier your new additions:

- **A-Tier:** Strong timing signal (lease expiring in 12–18 months), right company size, you have a warm connection or clear angle. These get your best effort.
- **B-Tier:** Good fit but weaker timing signal, or strong signal but smaller deal size. Still worth pursuing, but they get a lighter touch.
- **C-Tier:** Speculative. You think they might need space but don't have strong evidence. These go into automated or semi-automated sequences.

A realistic Monday split might look like: 3–4 A-tier, 6–8 B-tier, and 6–8 C-tier prospects added to your queue.

---

## Tuesday: Research + First Touches (1 Hour)

**8:00 AM – 8:50 AM: Research + Draft Outreach (10–12 Prospects)**

This is the most important hour of the week. You're turning names on a list into informed, personalized first touches.

For each of your A and B-tier prospects, spend about 5 minutes doing a quick research sweep:

- **Company website:** What do they do? How big are they? Any recent news on their blog or press page?
- **LinkedIn (the decision-maker):** What's their role? How long have they been there? Have they posted anything recently you can reference?
- **News search:** Any recent funding, expansion, leadership changes, or industry trends that affect them?
- **Current space:** Can you find their current address? Estimate their square footage? When does the lease likely expire?

Then draft a first-touch email that references something specific. Not "I noticed your lease might be expiring soon" — that's what every broker sends. Try: "Saw your team just opened 12 new positions in Chicago — congrats on the growth. When companies scale that fast, space usually becomes a constraint before they realize it. Worth a quick conversation?"

Stay disciplined about the 5-minute research cap. Don't go down a 20-minute rabbit hole on one company. Good enough context beats perfect context — you're starting a conversation, not writing a thesis.

**8:50 AM – 9:00 AM: LinkedIn Connects**

For each prospect you emailed, send a LinkedIn connection request. Keep the note short — one sentence, no pitch. "Hi [Name], I work with growing companies in [market] on their space strategy. Would love to connect." That's it.

When they see your email and a LinkedIn request the same day, it signals professionalism, not desperation.

---

## Wednesday: Phone Block (1 Hour)

**8:00 AM – 9:00 AM: Calls**

Wednesday is voice day. Prioritize your call list in this order:

1. **Prospects who opened your email but didn't reply.** Most email tools show open data. Someone who opened your email multiple times is interested — they just didn't act. Open with: "Hi [Name], I sent you a note yesterday about [topic]. Figured a quick call might be easier than going back and forth over email."

2. **Follow-ups from previous weeks.** Anyone who expressed even mild interest gets a call. "Last time we spoke you mentioned you weren't thinking about your space yet. Has anything changed?"

3. **Cold calls to A-tier prospects.** If you have A-tier targets you haven't reached yet, Wednesday is the day.

**Voicemail strategy:** Keep it under 30 seconds and reference your email: "Hi [Name], it's [Your Name] with [Brokerage]. I sent you a note about your team's growth in [market]. Had a couple of ideas on the space side — call me back at [number] or just reply to the email."

Aim for 15–20 dials in the hour. You'll connect with 3–5 people. That's normal. The voicemails reinforce your email and build familiarity.

---

## Thursday: Follow-Up Sequences (1 Hour)

**8:00 AM – 8:30 AM: Second and Third Touches**

Most deals are won in the follow-up, not the first touch. Go through your outreach sequences and send the next touch to everyone who's due:

- **Second touch (3–4 days after first):** Reference the original email and add new value — a market stat, a comp, a relevant article. "Following up on my note from Tuesday. [Submarket] asking rents moved up 8% last quarter, hitting tenants with renewals coming up. Happy to share more detail."

- **Third touch (5–7 days after second):** Switch channels or angles. If the first two were email, try LinkedIn. Try a direct question: "Are you planning to stay in your current space when your lease comes up, or is there any chance you'd explore options?"

**8:30 AM – 8:45 AM: Share Relevant Content**

Pick 3–5 prospects and send them something genuinely useful — not a newsletter blast, but a targeted share. A market report for their submarket. A news article about their industry. A deal comp showing what similar companies are paying. The best follow-ups don't feel like follow-ups.

**8:45 AM – 9:00 AM: LinkedIn Engagement**

Spend 15 minutes engaging with prospects' LinkedIn content. Leave thoughtful comments — not "Great post!" but observations that add to the conversation. If a prospect posted about hitting a growth milestone, comment with a smart take on what that growth usually means for space needs.

This is a slow-burn tactic. It doesn't book meetings today, but it keeps you visible — and when their lease comes up, you're the broker they already know.

---

## Friday: Measure + Plan (30 Minutes)

**8:00 AM – 8:30 AM: Review and Plan**

You can't improve what you don't measure. Spend Friday morning looking at the numbers from the week:

- **Emails sent:** You should be sending 30–50 per week (first touches plus follow-ups).
- **Open rate:** Below 30%? Your subject lines need work. Above 50%? Your messaging is landing.
- **Reply rate:** Aim for 8–15% on first touches. Higher on follow-ups.
- **Meetings booked:** This is the number that matters. Track it weekly and monthly.
- **Pipeline adds:** How many new real opportunities entered your pipeline?

Update your CRM. Every contact, every note, every next step. If it's not in the CRM, it didn't happen.

Then spend the last 10 minutes prepping for Monday. Flag data sources to pull, note trigger events you spotted during the week, and jot down referrals to follow up on. This setup means Monday's pipeline review starts clean instead of cold.

## Time-Saving Tips That Multiply Your Output

The routine above works as described. But there are a few tactics that can compress the time or amplify the results.

**Batch relentlessly.** The reason each day has a theme is that context-switching kills productivity. When you're in research mode, stay in research mode. When you're on the phone, don't stop to draft emails. Batching similar tasks can cut the time required by 30–40%.

**Build a template library, then customize.** Build 5–6 first-touch templates organized by prospect type (growth-stage company, upcoming expiration, new market entry). Then customize the first two sentences for each prospect. The template handles structure; your research handles personalization.

**Use AI to compress the research step.** This is the single biggest time-saver available right now. The Tuesday research phase — company info, decision-makers, trigger events, space needs — used to take 20–40 minutes per prospect. AI tools can synthesize that in seconds, delivering a prospect brief with everything you need. What used to be a 10-prospect morning becomes a 30-prospect morning. That changes the math on your entire pipeline.

**Leverage your commute.** Use drive time for your Wednesday phone block. Hands-free calls on the way in can knock out 5–8 dials before you sit down.

**Pair up with a colleague.** Find another broker who wants to build better habits and do your morning blocks at the same time. When someone else is counting on you to show up, you show up.

## Start Tomorrow

Here's the math: if you reach 40 new prospects per week and convert 5% into meetings, that's 2 meetings per week, 8 per month, 24 per quarter. If one in four becomes an active pursuit, you're adding 6 real opportunities per quarter. In tenant rep, where a single deal can be worth $50,000 to $500,000 in commission, that math gets real fast.

And the prospects you don't convert today don't disappear. They go into your nurture list. Six months from now, when their lease situation changes, you're the broker who's been consistently showing up. You get the call.

Open your calendar right now and block five morning hours for next week. Label them "Prospecting — Do Not Move." It won't feel natural the first week. By week three, it becomes a habit. By week eight, you'll see it in your pipeline. By month six, you'll wonder how you ever operated without it.

The routine is the competitive advantage. Now go build it.
